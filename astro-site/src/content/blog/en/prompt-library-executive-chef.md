<p>Executive Chef Pro is the AI Sous Chef for the corner office. It helps executive chefs, kitchen managers, and owner-operators translate culinary vision into a business plan that runs tight on numbers without crushing creativity. From engineering a menu matrix that boosts contribution margin, to costing a dish with real shrinkage on the prep sheet, to drafting brigade station guides and HACCP-based SOPs, this agent builds the operational backbone that lets you lead the line and the P&L at the same time. It serves as your strategic partner for annual planning, KPI dashboards, and the conversations with ownership where a wrong food-cost decimal can cost you a capital request.</p>
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why prompts matter with Executive Chef Pro</h2>
<p>Ask any AI for a menu suggestion and you’ll get a list of dishes. Ask it to engineer a menu with an average 32% food cost, a minimum 68% gross profit per item, and cross-utilization of proteins across three stations — and you get a profit engine. Executive Chef Pro’s output is only as sharp as the constraints you feed it. If you don’t define your target plate cost, acceptable trim loss, or the reality that your prep cooks have 90 minutes to mise out a station, the agent will assume a blank-slate kitchen that doesn’t exist.</p>
<p>Precise prompts turn the agent from a recipe generator into a decision-support tool. When you include your actual yield percentages from butcher tests, your supplier price book, and the HACCP flow for a new menu item, the agent builds costing sheets that mirror your kitchen’s reality. Vague prompts produce generic “leadership advice”; targeted prompts generate brigade-specific station checklists, talking points for pre-shift, and corrective-action plans that respect your existing SOPs. For an executive chef, the difference between a prompt that saves you an hour of spreadsheet work and one that wastes ten minutes is all in the numbers you give it.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">OPTIMIZE WITH TARGETS</h3>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">SPECIFY YOUR BRIGADE</h3>
<p>State your team size and skill level so procedures match your reality. Example: "We're a 12-cook brigade with medium turnover. Give me an SOP for fish station mise en place that a commis can follow."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">INCLUDE MARKET CONTEXT</h3>
<p>Mention your location and guest profile to sharpen recommendations. Example: "Restaurant in Austin, mix of tourists and locals, $45 average check." For UK/ANZ/India, name the city — the agent adapts sourcing and pricing cues.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">REQUEST REAL YIELDS</h3>
<p>Always ask for yield percentage per ingredient, not just net weight, for accurate cost cards. Example: "Cost a roast chicken for 6 covers, factoring in deboning waste and cooking loss."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">ITERATE IN STAGES</h3>
<p>For complex projects, break the work into steps: cost card first, then tech sheet, then SOP. Example: "Give me the tuna tartare cost card. Once I validate it, we'll build the full spec sheet."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">USE ENGINEERING REFERENCES</h3>
<p>Ask for menu-engineering analysis using the popularity–profitability matrix. Example: "Analyze my current starter menu and classify the 8 plates into stars, plowhorses, puzzles and dogs."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">BALANCE QUALITY AND COST</h3>
