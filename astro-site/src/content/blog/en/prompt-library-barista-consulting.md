<p>Barista Consultant Pro gives specialty-coffee operators a virtual green-buyer, Q grader and menu engineer in one tool. Whether you're a roastery sourcing containers from Huila or a multi-unit cafe dialing in a seasonal single-origin espresso, this agent translates cupping notes, farm data and cost sheets into decisions you can execute on the cupping table and the P&L. <h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why prompts matter with Barista Consultant Pro</h2>
<p>Specialty coffee is a low-margin, high-variability business where a one-point shift in extraction or a $0.25/lb swing in green price can wipe out profit. A generic prompt like “suggest a coffee for my café” yields a chatty blog post. A precise prompt—specifying roast degree, target TDS, extraction yield, price ceiling, flavor callouts, and equipment—forces the agent to think like a working green buyer and head roaster, spitting out actionable specs, not adjectives.</p>
<p>In the prompt library below, each entry mimics a real buying or barista-ops conversation. You’ll tell the agent your basket dose, your group-head temp, your water chemistry, the cupping scores you require, and the cost-of-goods ceiling. That’s how you get a recommended lot with a sample-roast profile, a dial-in recipe, and a printed beverage-cost breakdown that accounts for waste and milk—data your shift lead can use right now. Vague prompts get you a coffee description; precise prompts get you a purchase order and a brew sheet.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Tips for getting more out of Barista Consultant Pro</h2>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Origin & Process</h3>
<p>Always specify country, region, variety, and processing method (washed, natural, honey, anaerobic). We use this to recommend precise cup profiles, extraction parameters, and recipe targets. For example, a natural Ethiopia Sidamo demands a different approach than a washed Colombian.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Equipment Specs</h3>
<p>Specify your espresso machine, grinder (e.g., Mazzer, EK43, Mythos), and brew method. The gear type—like a double-boiler Synesso with flat burrs—directly dictates optimal dose, grind size, temperature stability, and extraction parameters. Always include model and burr type for precise calibration.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Measurable Parameters</h3>
<p>Provide exact numbers: dose (g), yield (g), ratio, extraction time (sec), brew temp (°F), and target TDS or EY%. Example: “18 g dose, 36 g yield, 1:2 ratio, 26 sec at 200°F.” These metrics let us reverse-engineer your recipe and fine-tune flavor balance.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Budget Constraints</h3>
<p>State green coffee cost per pound, maximum beverage cost percentage, and selling price. Knowing your cost structure ensures the protocol stays profitable. Economic viability hinges on these numbers—e.g., “max beverage cost 12% for a $3.50 espresso.” Include labor and overhead if you need a true net margin.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Diagnostic Info</h3>
<p>Describe extraction defects precisely (sour, bitter, thin body, channeling) for adjustment guidance. Example: “shot pulls fast, tastes harsh and astringent” helps pinpoint grind, ratio, or distribution fixes. Specify if the issue occurs with all shots or intermittently, and whether puck prep is consistent for an accurate diagnosis.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Cost Breakdown</h3>
<p>Request a full cost analysis: green price/lb, roast loss, yield per cup, cost per drink, and gross margin. Example: “calculate beverage cost for a 20 g dose espresso shot.” This breakdown lets you price menus sustainably and compare green coffee options.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Milk Alternative</h3>
<p>For milk-based drinks, name the exact product (whole milk, barista oat, almond) so we tweak steaming temp and texture. Example: “with Oatly Barista Edition.” Different alternatives scorch or stretch differently; specifying brand and fat content ensures proper microfoam and temperature control.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Water Chemistry</h3>
<p>Water is 98% of the brew. Share TDS (ppm), hardness, alkalinity, and pH to dial in extraction and flavor balance. Example: “water at 100 ppm TDS, 60 ppm KH.” If using a filtration system, note the brand and cartridge type; we can suggest adjustments for SCA spec water.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Training Plan</h3>
<p>Request structured barista training with objectives, hours, competency checks, and materials. Example: “design a 20-hour curriculum for junior baristas covering dial-in, latte art, and workflow.” Tell me current skill levels and whether you need on-bar or classroom focus; I’ll build a plan with tasting protocols and efficiency drills.</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Menu Engineering</h3>
<p>For menu design, define positioning, customer demo, target average ticket, and number of offerings. Example: “6 single-origin pour-overs for a downtown shop, average ticket $4.50.” Mention if you serve pastries or seasonal specials—menu mix affects profitability and workflow.
