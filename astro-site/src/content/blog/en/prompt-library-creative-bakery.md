<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why prompts matter with Creative Bakery</h2>
<p>In baking, a prompt that says “give me a sourdough loaf” is useless. A prompt that specifies 80% hydration, 20% whole-grain levain, a 24-hour cold bulk, and a deep mahogany crust with an open, glossy crumb gives the agent the constraints it needs to generate a baker’s formula, a scaled production recipe, and a cost sheet that reflects actual flour, labor, and energy. Without that precision, you get a generic internet recipe that ignores your oven, your flour, and your margin. A precise prompt turns the agent into a silent sous chef who knows your formula book.</p>
<p>The same logic applies to laminated doughs: “a croissant with 82% butter, 3-4-3 fold, 16-hour cold retard” yields a dough schedule that fits your shift, calculates butter cost per unit, and flags potential lamination faults. For a bakery that runs 300 units a day, the difference between a vague prompt and a detailed one is a few hundred dollars in wasted butter and a day’s lost production. This agent doesn’t just spit out recipes; it ties creative ambition to the P&L, so you can afford to experiment.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Tips for getting more out of Creative Bakery</h2>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Define a reference baker style</h3>

<p>State the water percentage relative to total flour so crumb texture hits your target exactly. Example: “I want a whole wheat pan loaf at 90% hydration with a very tender crumb.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Include sourdough type and percentage</h3>
<p>Define whether the levain is liquid or stiff and its proportion to total flour to dial in acidity and fermentation speed. Example: “Use 30% stiff levain at 50% hydration.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Request dough temperature curve</h3>
<p>Ask for water temperature and final dough temp to match your bakery conditions. Example: “Calculate water temperature for a DDT of 77°F with flour at 68°F and ambient at 72°F.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Request full baking schedule</h3>
<p>Detail initial steam, venting times, and exact temperatures for the crust you want. Example: “Bake with steam first 18 min at 465°F, then 30 min at 390°F with vent open.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Use city-specific cost breakdown</h3>
<p>Get cost per piece and per pound including waste and byproduct recipes. Example: “Cost breakdown for a butter croissant in Chicago with 8% waste.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Integrate alternative ingredients with notes</h3>
<p>When using gluten-free flours or pseudocereals, add a brief note on dough behavior. Example: “Formulate a gluten-free bread with teff flour, which holds little water and dries out easily.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Request editorial image style</h3>
<p>Describe angle, light, and props for a professional product shot. Example: “Top-down shot of the loaf on a dark wood board, serrated knife alongside, crumb exposed.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Request PDF or CSV export</h3>
<p>Choose the format to share with your team or feed into your management software. Example: “Download the formulation and cost breakdown as CSV to import into my ERP.”</p>
