<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why prompts matter with Staff Meal</h2>
<p>A vague prompt like “give me staff meal ideas” gets you a generic list that ignores what’s actually in your walk-in. That means wasted trim, blown budgets, and a crew that orders DoorDash on the sly. Staff Meal is built to work the other way: you tell it what you have, and it reverse-engineers dishes from there. A precise prompt — naming your surplus proteins, the bases already prepped, and a hard cost ceiling — forces the agent to think like a kitchen manager, not a recipe blog. You get menus that move product before it spoils and keep food cost below thirty percent.</p>
<p>The second reason precision matters is consistency across shifts. When the morning sous and the night lead are working off the same prompt template, the agent produces standardized recipe cards with yields, portion weights, and allergen callouts. That means the guy covering Tuesday’s meal isn’t winging it with a different food cost than the person who cooked Monday. In a domain where a dollar a cover overages forty people five days a week is eight grand a year, the right prompt pays for itself before the first sheet pan hits the oven.</p>
