<p>Allergen ID breaks down any recipe, ingredient, or processed product to surface both direct and hidden allergens. It goes well beyond rattling off the FDA’s nine major food allergens: the agent analyzes spec sheets, intermediate prep steps, and commercial labels, evaluates real cross-contact risks on your line, and suggests safe substitutions that hold up under heat, texture, and flavor. Whether you’re writing a new menu, training the crew, or dealing with a last-minute supplier swap, Allergen ID gives you the granular intel to make confident decisions—so you can protect guests without compromising the plate.</p>
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why prompts matter with Allergen ID</h2>
<p>Precision matters because allergen management is binary: a dish is safe or it isn’t. A vague prompt like “check this recipe for allergens” might return a flat list, but it won’t reveal that the fryer oil shares a vat with shellfish or that the spice blend contains undeclared milk powder. A tight prompt—naming the specific allergen, the cooking station, and holding conditions—forces Allergen ID to analyze like a chef on the line. For instance, “Analyze this miso-glazed cod for fish, soy, and wheat cross-contact, including the grill, marinade brush, and garnish tweezers” yields a HACCP-minded breakdown of where contamination could occur and what to change.</p>
<p>Good prompts also help when supplier data is thin. Asking about “natural flavors” or “spices” can push the agent to flag hidden allergens, suggest manufacturer questions, or cross-reference industry norms. You can steer the output toward your regulatory framework—FDA, UK FSA, FSANZ—so the advice aligns with your labeling duties. In short, the prompt turns Allergen ID from a simple lookup into a real-time risk assessor.</p>
