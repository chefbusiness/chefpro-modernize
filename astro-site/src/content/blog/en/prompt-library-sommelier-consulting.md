<p>Sommelier Consultant Pro is a wine-industry AI built for the demands of American kitchens. It helps chefs, somms, and owners build a wine portfolio by region and style, compare vintages across Old and New World producers, and pinpoint technical problems like cork taint, oxidation, or cost overruns. It generates ready-to-use documents: wine lists organized by glass and bottle, decreasing-markup costing sheets that show your beverage cost at a glance, and pairing suggestions that weave the list into the menu. If you’re opening a new spot in Portland or revamping a legacy list in Chicago, this agent gives you the practical, operational advice you need, not textbook fluff.</p>
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why prompts matter with Sommelier Consultant Pro</h2>
<p>Wine programs live and die by numbers that change with every bottle. A loose prompt that asks for a “wine list” might yield a random selection of labels, but a tight prompt that specifies the budget per category, the dining concept (steakhouse vs. oyster bar), and the markup policy—say, a higher percentage on entry-level BTG pours and a sliding scale on reserve bottles—forces Sommelier Consultant Pro to calculate exact pour costs, retail markups, and a realistic beverage cost target. Without that level of detail, you’ll get a list that looks pretty but bleeds margin.</p>
<p>The same precision applies to food pairing. A vague request for “pairings with salmon” delivers boilerplate Pinot Noir; a sharp prompt that names the preparation (cedar-planked, miso-glazed), side vegetables, and the price point of the by-the-glass program generates actionable matches that work for your own menu. In a world where a 2 % swing in beverage cost can make or break the quarterly P&L, the difference between a fuzzy prompt and a chef-driven request is the difference between a useful wine tool and a novelty.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Tips for getting more out of Sommelier Consultant Pro</h2>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">ESTABLISHMENT PROFILE</h3>
<p>Always open by asking for the restaurant type, average cover count and current wine average check before designing any list. "What's the concept—independent, hotel, group? Current wine per-cover average and overall beverage cost target?"</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">BEVERAGE COST TARGET</h3>
<p>Lock in the target beverage cost and minimum gross margin from the jump. "I need a list that runs a 32% beverage cost max with a minimum 65% gross margin on every bottle sold."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">KITCHEN DEEP DIVE</h3>
<p>The more you share about the culinary program, the sharper the pairings. "Chef is running French technique with hyper-local Galician product. Lead proteins: percebes, whole lobster, Leonese lamb."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">REAL-TIME MARKET PRICING</h3>
<p>Always request current wholesale pricing and verify availability before committing to a reference. "What's your current landed cost on Vega Sicilia Único 2012 for a six-bottle drop?"</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">STYLE-DRIVEN STRUCTURE</h3>
<p>Organize the list by style categories—sparkling, white, red, fortified—not just by geography. "Build the card by style: Sparkling, Whites, Reds, Dessert, Fortified. Sub-sections by region within each."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">ENTRY-LEVEL HIGH-MARGIN ANCHOR</h3>
<p>Include an entry-level reference in every category that delivers strong margin. "In Spanish reds, anchor with a Bierzo Mencía at a $4.50 purchase cost running a 4x markup."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">NEGOTIATION STRATEGY</h3>
<p>Ask for concrete negotiation angles with distributors for exclusives or preferential terms. "What terms can I push with the Torres rep to lock up exclusivity on Mirador in my zip code?"</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">STAFF TRAINING SCRIPTS</h3>
<p>Request role-play scenarios and guided tasting scripts to train the floor team. "Generate a six-wine guided tasting script to train the team on Ribera del Duero reds."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">SUSTAINABILITY INTEGRATION</h3>
<p>Factor in natural, biodynamic and small-producer wines as a deliberate section. "Build a natural wine section with at least five references from small producers—low-intervention, biodynamic preferred."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">SEASONAL REFRESH</h3>
<p>Request seasonal updates to the list based on availability and menu shifts. "Refresh the white list for spring: lead with young Godello, Albariño and Verdejo.
