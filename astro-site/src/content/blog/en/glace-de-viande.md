Glace de viande is brown stock reduced to a syrup. In Le Guide Culinaire (1903), Auguste Escoffier calls it ready when it coats a spoon in a shining layer that stays put. The heat drops as the stock concentrates. Hot, it is a dark syrup. Cold, it sets, because the gelatin from the bones, once concentrated, firms up. The French name means meat glaze.

## What glace de viande is

You start with brown stock that is already strained and unsalted. You evaporate water until what remains is mostly flavor and gelatin. There is no flour. Hot, it shines and falls from the spoon in slow, wide drops. Cold, it behaves like a firm jelly.

[Demi-glace](https://aichef.pro/en/blog/what-is-demi-glace) stops earlier. It contains espagnole, so it has roux and tomato, and it is finished with a fortified wine. If someone offers you "demi" and, once cold, it sets into a hard dark block with no flour taste, what you have is a glaze, or a demi made from stock alone.

## Where it comes from

Escoffier makes the glaze by reducing the brown stock he calls estouffade. As the volume drops, he strains the liquid through cloth and moves it into smaller pans. The heat, lively at the start, has to be gentle at the end. With little water left, a hard boil scorches it.

The spoon tells you when it is done. The glaze is ready when it wraps the spoon in a shining, well-stuck coat. He puts it in one verb: it should nap the spoon, "qu'elle nappe la cuiller." When he wants a lighter, clearer glaze, the same text says to reduce ordinary veal stock.

## Ingredients and ratios

There is no table that says "this many quarts become one cup." The yield depends on the gelatin in the stock.

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Brown stock or veal stock, unsalted and strained | whatever you have, for example 2 liters (about 8 1/2 cups) |
| Salt | none until the end, and you will rarely need it |

Two liters is a sensible size at home. In a restaurant you start from the day's pot. If the stock is cloudy or fatty, clean it first. Fat does not become glaze. It sits on top and turns bitter if it burns.

## How to make it, step by step

<figure><img src="/blog-assets/2026/05/fondo-oscuro-cuerpo.jpg" alt="Cold meat glaze set to a soft gel in a shallow tray in the fridge, a spoon showing the clean cut surface." loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:8px;margin:24px 0;" /></figure>

**1. Degrease and strain the stock.** It should be cold, so you can lift off the solidified fat, and it should be unsalted.

**2. Reduce it in a wide pan.** Medium heat at first, skimming. When it has dropped by half, strain it into a smaller pan.

**3. Lower the heat.** In the last stage the liquid is thick and catches quickly. You want a simmer that barely moves the surface. Test with a clean spoon. It is done when the metal comes out coated and the coat does not run off in a watery thread.

**4. Cool it and store it.** Pour it into a shallow container. It sets as it cools. Cut it into portions.

## Why it fails and how to fix it

If it scorches on the bottom of the pan, it turns bitter, and that bitterness goes through the whole glaze. Sugar and wine will not hide it. Discard what tastes burnt. Next time, move to a smaller pan and lower the heat sooner.

If you reduce and reduce and it is still liquid when cold, the stock had little gelatin: not enough bone, not enough cooking, or a thin broth from bones that had already been boiled out. Adding bones halfway through will not catch you up. Make a stock with real bone, shin or knuckle, and reduce that.

If it is salty, the stock already was. There is no clean fix.

## During service

Make it ahead. Cold, it takes little space and keeps better than the stock it came from. During service, melt the portion you need over a water bath, without boiling it. A hard boil dries it and can turn it bitter.

A teaspoon, melted into a thin sauce, puts body and flavor back. Brushing a roast with hot glaze gives it shine. Escoffier also counts, among its uses, mounting it with butter or cream and then serving it as a sauce.

<figure><img src="/blog-assets/2026/05/maillard-cuerpo.jpg" alt="A cook stirring a dark glaze at the last stage of reduction, the volume low in the pan, eyes on the heat." loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:8px;margin:24px 0;" /></figure>

In the refrigerator, well covered, use it within a few days. Frozen in small portions, it keeps much longer. If it smells sour or stale once thawed, do not try to save it.

## Variations

**Veal glaze.** The same work with veal stock. It comes out clearer and more neutral. Escoffier recommends it when that is the result he wants.

**Poultry or game glaze.** The same idea with the stock of that animal. Each one tastes of what you reduced. Do not swap them without tasting.

**Jus de veau lié.** Escoffier writes it on the same page. It is 4 liters of veal stock reduced to 1 liter, thickened with 30 g of arrowroot dispersed in cold stock. It contains starch, and when it cools it stays a sauce. A meat glaze sets.

## What to use it for

To strengthen sauces and jus, to shine a roast, and to keep the flavor of a long stock in one spoonful, without storing quarts of broth. It does not stand in for a [fish fumet](https://aichef.pro/en/blog/fish-fumet). The glaze is meat. The fumet is bones and a short cook.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is glace de viande a demi-glace?**
They are two different reductions. Escoffier's demi-glace starts from espagnole and brown stock, and is finished with sherry or Madeira. A meat glaze is stock alone, reduced to a syrup, with no flour and no wine.

**Why does it gel when it cools?**
During the long cooking of the stock, collagen in the bones turns into gelatin. Reduction concentrates that gelatin. In the cold, gelatin sets and the glaze can be cut. Heat melts it again.

**How far do you reduce it?**
Escoffier does not give a fixed fraction. He says stop when the glaze wraps the spoon in a shining, clinging coat. Depending on the stock, that happens sooner or later. A thin stock can reduce a long way and still never set.

**Can you use salted broth?**
It is a poor start. Escoffier wants unsalted stock, because reduction concentrates everything in it. Salted broth becomes inedible before it reaches a syrup. A very thin broth, with little gelatin, may never set no matter how far you reduce it.

**Do you serve it on its own?**
By the spoonful, yes, melted over a roast, or to strengthen a sauce. Cold and in a block, it is a kitchen ingredient. You melt a spoonful when you cook. Escoffier also uses it, once mounted with butter or cream, as a sauce.

**How do you pronounce glace de viande?**
Roughly glahss duh vee-ahnd. The final s in glace is silent.
