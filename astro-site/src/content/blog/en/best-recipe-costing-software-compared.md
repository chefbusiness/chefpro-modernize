<h2>What to Look for in Recipe Costing Software</h2>
<p>Before comparing tools, decide which features matter for your kitchen. Good <strong>food costing software</strong> should cover most of these:</p>
<h3>Core Features Checklist</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ingredient price updates:</strong> Change a supplier price once and see every affected dish update, ideally with an alert when a price jumps</li>
<li><strong>Sub-recipe support:</strong> Cost mother recipes (sauces, stocks, bases) once and carry that cost into every dish that uses them</li>
<li><strong>Yield and waste tracking:</strong> Account for trim loss, shrinkage and spoilage, so the cost reflects what reaches the plate</li>
<li><strong>POS integration:</strong> Connect to your point of sale to compare actual and theoretical food cost</li>
<li><strong>Multi-location sync:</strong> Keep the same recipes and costs across several restaurants or kitchens</li>
<li><strong>Allergen and nutrition labelling:</strong> Generate allergen and nutrition information straight from the recipe</li>
<li><strong>Reporting:</strong> Cost trends and reports you can export and share</li>
<li><strong>Mobile access:</strong> Check and update recipes from the kitchen on a phone or tablet</li>
<li><strong>AI support:</strong> Suggestions for menu changes and pricing, and a quick read on how a price rise affects each dish</li>
</ul>
<p>Prioritize the features that solve the problems you actually have. A single-site café needs far less than a 20-unit restaurant group, so pick something that fits today and can grow with you.</p>
