<p>The same precision matters for ingredient selection and costing. A prompt that only mentions “fresh mozzarella” might yield a generic suggestion. <h2 class="wp-block-heading">Tips for getting more out of Pizza Consultant Pro</h2>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Define Your Pizza Style Up Front</h3>
<p>Start by naming your style—Neapolitan, Roman al taglio, pinsa, or contemporary—to avoid mismatched formulations. Specifying style up front guarantees correct hydration, flour strength, and mixing method. Example: "Let's formulate a Roman al taglio dough with 70% hydration."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Specify Flour W-Value</h3>
<p>Always choose your flour's W-value (250–450) to match the dough's intended strength. This ensures your formula adapts to your actual flour, not a generic spec. Example: "Use a W 350 Manitoba flour for a Neapolitan dough that needs a 12-hour room-temperature ferment."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Separate Dough and Topping Costing</h3>
<p>Always request a split cost breakdown: dough ingredients vs. toppings. This yields precise menu pricing per pie, protects your food cost margin, and lets you adjust pricing as ingredient costs fluctuate. Example: "Calculate dough cost and topping cost separately for a $14.99 Margherita pizza."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Spell Out Fermentation Schedules</h3>
<p>Tell me if you run a same-day production, bulk ferment, or cold retardation process. This lets me tailor the yeast percentage and timing to your workflow. Example: "I need a protocol for a 48-hour cold-fermented dough with 0.1% fresh yeast."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Mention Your Oven Setup</h3>
<p>Describe your oven: wood-fired, electric deck, or convection. I'll adjust temperature and bake time; a 900°F wood-fired oven bakes in 60-90 seconds, while a 550°F deck takes 6-8 minutes. Example: "I'm running a Moretti Fiammetta wood-fired oven at 900°F."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Specify Dough Ball Weights</h3>
<p>For production scaling, give me the dough ball weight you need (9-12 oz) and the number of pies per batch. This standardizes your line and controls portion costs. Example: "I want 10 oz balls to make 15 pizzas per batch."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Define Your Target Market</h3>
<p>Describe your guest profile: family-friendly, fast-casual urban, or premium dining. Your market determines whether you run a high-volume slice joint or a sit-down Neapolitan destination, calibrating menu pricing and complexity. Example: "Menu for an urban spot, guests 25-45, average check $15."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Request DOP Ingredients for Authenticity</h3>
<p>Always specify DOP ingredients when aiming for true Neapolitan style, like buffalo mozzarella di bufala Campana and San Marzano tomatoes. This guarantees origin, quality, and a menu that can command premium pricing. Example: "Use DOP buffalo mozzarella for the Margherita."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Request Menu Variations</h3>
<p>Ask for at least three options per category—classics, specialties, and signature pies—to balance offering, food cost, and prep efficiency. This variety keeps the menu interesting without overcomplicating the line. Example: "Build a menu with 4 classics, 3 specialties, and 2 chef's creations."</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Ask for the Bake Protocol</h3>
<p>Always request time, temperature, and oven positioning for each style. A Neapolitan pie needs 60-90 seconds at 900°F on the stone; a pan pizza might need 15 minutes at 475°F on the middle rack.
