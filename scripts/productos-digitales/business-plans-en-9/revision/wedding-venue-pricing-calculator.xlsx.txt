## Instructions
A1: HOW TO USE — Wedding Venue Pricing & Quote Calculator
A2: It turns your rate card into a full quote for a specific event: the season and type of day of the date, the guest minimum, extra services, sales tax, total, payment schedule, the estimated caterer commission, the event contribution and what you keep if the client cancels. Type only in the GREEN cells; the margin is calculated.
A3: 1. Choose the client (Couple or Corporate), the event type, the full date and the expected guests.
A4: 2. The date sets the season (month table) and the type of day (weekday table). Each one carries a premium or a discount in its own visible table; both multiply the event type rate.
A5: 3. Each season has a guest minimum: the quote uses the higher of the expected guests and the minimum, and the commission is estimated on that guaranteed guest count.
A6: 4. Type how many extra services the event takes (ceremony on site, additional hours, premium decor package, rehearsal the day before). Their prices are in the "Venue extra services" table and are Kit examples: type your own.
A7: 5. Type the venue staff hours, the hourly wage, the payroll tax rate and the direct cost of the extras. The direct event cost (cleaning, restrooms, generator fuel, parking and security) is in the event type table.
A8: SALES TAX, COMMISSION AND THE CATERER'S MENU
A10: The venue bills its rental fee and services at the sales tax rate you type (8% in the example). Whether a venue rental fee is taxable depends on the state (taxable in some, exempt in others, sometimes only when it is billed with catering): ask your state's department of revenue. The exclusive caterer bills food and the bar with its own sales tax, service charge and gratuity; they are not venue revenue, and the calculator shows them only as a reference for the client.
A11: The caterer commission is what the exclusive caterer pays the venue for the exclusivity. Here it is an editable assumption (10% of the caterer's food & beverage sales, net of sales tax, service charge and gratuities): it is not a market figure, negotiate it. The caterer pays it, not the client; the plan treats it as a fee between businesses (0% sales tax in the example): confirm it with your CPA.
A12: The event contribution does not deduct rent, salaried staff, utilities, insurance or overhead. Those are in the financial plan.
A13: PAYMENTS AND CANCELLATION
A15: Three payments: the retainer at signing, a second installment and the final balance before the event, each with its due date. The retainer is applied to the price. Issue a receipt for every payment. Couple: 25%, 35% six months before and 40% 30 days before. Corporate: 30%, 30% two months before and 40% 15 days before. These are Kit examples.
A16: Cancellation is written in tiers by months, with no gaps: the first starts at 12 months or more and the last runs to the event day. The amounts kept rise as the date gets closer and should be a reasonable estimate of the venue's loss, not a penalty: the agreement credits back what you recover by rebooking the date. Never write "non-refundable" without the schedule. If the venue cancels, it refunds everything it collected. Have an attorney in your state review the schedule (the kit's Venue Rental Agreement Templates use the same tiers).
A17: If you sign couples at bridal shows or off-site, ask your attorney whether the FTC Cooling-Off Rule or your state's home-solicitation law applies; many venues give a short cancellation window for show-floor signings anyway.
A18: KIT ESTIMATES
A20: The default values and the three examples are estimates to show the calculation, not market prices. Replace them with your rates, extras, costs and tiers before you quote.
