A new product just landed in our English store: Catering Business Plan Kit. It's for anyone who wants to start an event catering company — corporate lunches and receptions during the week, weddings and private parties on weekends — working from a shared commissary kitchen, and needs to know whether it works on paper before buying a van.

It's 4 files: a 10-section business plan in Word, an Excel financial model with 9 sheets, a pricing calculator and a 68-task startup checklist, set up for the US with notes for the UK.

Four things you'll use from day one:

  • A plan that's already written. Executive summary, concept, market and competition, marketing, operations, staffing, financials, permits and an action plan, written for a full-service event caterer in the format lenders expect. Its figures are the ones in the Excel model.
  • Your numbers before you buy the van. Startup costs line by line, from the refrigerated van and its cooling unit to chafing dishes and first-year permits, a 3-year P&L driven by events per year, price per guest and guests per event, break-even in events per year, three scenarios, event staff on W-2 payroll with employer payroll taxes and a 12-month cash flow with quarterly sales tax.
  • A price for every quote. The pricing calculator turns food cost per guest, staff hours, rentals and mileage into a price per guest, adds the service charge, the sales tax and the deposit, and shows the gross margin the quote really leaves, with the voluntary gratuity kept off the price.
  • The kitchen and the permits first. 68 tasks in 6 phases: LLC and EIN, seller's permit and business license, a written commissary agreement, the county catering permit, temporary event permits, commercial auto and liquor liability insurance, W-2 event staff and alcohol server certification, The Knot, WeddingWire and ezCater, up to your first 90 days.

The loan schedule and the DSCR (checked against a 1.25× target) cover your main loan, from a bank or SBA 7(a); the financing sheet also lists an SBA Microloan, investors and local grants as other sources of funds. Plus two bonuses: a Catering Permits & Licenses Guide (US + UK notes) and US catering benchmarks, from food cost and labor to price per guest and shared kitchen rates, with their sources.

The files are Microsoft Excel and Word, set up to print on US Letter. The checklist is a starting point (requirements vary by state, county and city), and the kit is a planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice: review the final numbers with your accountant before you apply for a loan.
