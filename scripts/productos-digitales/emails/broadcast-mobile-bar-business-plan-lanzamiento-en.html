A new product just landed in our English store: Mobile Bar Business Plan Kit. It’s for anyone who wants to start a mobile bar — weddings, company parties and private celebrations — working dry hire (your client buys the alcohol; you bring the bar, the bartenders and everything else), and needs to know whether it works on paper before buying a trailer.

It’s 4 files: a 10-section business plan in Word, an Excel financial model with 9 sheets, a pricing calculator and a 68-task startup checklist, set up for the US with notes for the UK.

Four things you’ll use from day one:

  • A plan that’s already written. Executive summary, concept, market and competition, marketing, operations, staffing, financials, permits and an action plan, written for a dry hire mobile bar with a bar trailer and a portable bar, in the format lenders expect. Its figures are the ones in the Excel model, and it’s honest about them: the example is a full-time operator with two crews, in the upper quarter of the market.
  • Your numbers before you buy the trailer. Startup costs line by line, from the used bar trailer and its conversion to glassware, coolers and server certifications, a 3-year P&L driven by events per year, price per guest and guests per event, break-even in events per year, three scenarios, W-2 bartenders and barbacks with employer payroll taxes and a 12-month cash flow with quarterly sales tax.
  • A price for every quote. The pricing calculator turns staff hours, mixers, garnish, ice, cups and mileage into a price per guest and its hourly equivalent, adds the service charge, the sales tax and the deposit, shows the gross margin the quote really leaves and builds the shopping list your client takes to the store. Ten signature cocktail specs included.
  • Your state’s alcohol rules first. 68 tasks in 6 phases: LLC and EIN, seller’s permit and business license, your state’s answer on a bartending-only service in writing, the county health department on garnish and ice, general and liquor liability with the certificates of insurance venues ask for, the trailer at the DMV, W-2 bartenders with TABC, RBS or TIPS certification, The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola and GigSalad, up to your first 90 days.

The loan schedule and the DSCR (checked against a 1.25× target) cover your main loan, from a bank or SBA 7(a); the financing sheet also lists an SBA Microloan, investors and local grants as other sources of funds. Plus two bonuses: a Mobile Bar Insurance, Permits & Venue Requirements Guide (US + UK notes) and US mobile bar benchmarks, from price per event and bartender ratios to the real first year of a new mobile bar, with their sources.

The files are Microsoft Excel and Word, set up to print on US Letter. Alcohol rules vary by state, so the checklist starts by asking yours, and the kit is a planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice: review the final numbers with your accountant before you apply for a loan.
