A new product just landed in our English store: Wedding Venue Business Plan Kit. It’s for anyone who wants to open a wedding and event venue on a leased barn or estate, with an exclusive caterer, and needs to know whether it works on paper before signing a lease or starting the build-out.

It’s 5 files: a 10-section business plan in Word, an Excel financial model with 9 sheets, a pricing and quote calculator, venue rental agreement templates and an 84-task startup checklist, set up for the US with notes for the UK.

Four things you’ll use from day one:

  • A plan that’s already written. Executive summary, concept, market and competition, marketing, operations, staffing, financials, permits and licenses, and an action plan, written for a barn or estate venue in the format lenders expect. Its figures are the ones in the Excel model, and it’s honest about them: a net margin of about 9% in year 1 with rent and a loan in the numbers.
  • Your numbers, with the Saturdays in them. Startup costs line by line, from the architect’s drawings, the conditional use permit and the sprinklers to the restrooms and the caterer’s prep kitchen, a 3-year P&L driven by events per year, guests and revenue per guest, break-even in events, three scenarios, and a 12-month cash flow that starts in March, so you see the June and September-October peaks and the winter trough before you sign the lease.
  • A calculator that prices your dates, and the agreements. Choose the client, the event type and the date, and the sheet sets the season and the day of the week, adds the extras and gives you the price with sales tax, the payment schedule and your caterer commission. Three editable agreements (couples, corporate clients and the exclusive caterer) come with a retainer and a cancellation schedule. They are templates, not legal advice: have a local attorney review them.
  • An 84-task startup checklist. Six phases: LLC and EIN, the zoning check before you sign the lease, a lease contingent on the permit, the fire marshal and the building official in writing, the exclusive caterer agreement, bridal shows, your open house and your first season of events.

Plus three bonuses: a Zoning, Permits & Fire Code Guide (US + UK notes), US wedding venue benchmarks, each with its source or marked as a kit estimate, and the US · UK · Canada · Australia Equivalents Guide.

The files are Microsoft Excel and Word, set up to print on US Letter. Zoning, fire code and alcohol rules vary by county and state, so the plan tells you to ask yours in writing, and the kit is a planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice: review the final numbers with your accountant before you apply for a loan.
