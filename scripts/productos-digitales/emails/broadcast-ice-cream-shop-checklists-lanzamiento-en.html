A new kit just landed in our English store: Ice Cream Shop Checklists Kit Pro. It’s for owners, ice cream makers and shift leads of shops that make their own ice cream or gelato and are tired of repeating the same instructions every shift: a dipping cabinet switched off overnight with every pan inside, a mix aged too warm, the same scoop going from pistachio to vanilla, a temperature nobody logged and a summer crew that doesn’t know where to start. The tasks are already written: you adjust them to your shop, print, delegate and file.

It’s 11 Excel checklists with 298 ice cream shop tasks, sorted by shift, area and role. Every task has its area, who does it and the time, and the counter on each list skips the tasks you mark N/A. Temperatures are in °F under the FDA Food Code (frozen storage at 0 °F or below, dipping cabinets at 6–10 °F during service, the mix aged at 36–39 °F and never above 41 °F), and every sheet prints on US Letter.

Inside the kit:

  • Opening and closing: the dipping cabinets, toppings, the counter and the allergen chart, plus a whole-shop checklist (alarm, lights, HVAC, the patio, the cabinets’ night covers and closing temperatures before the lock-up) and a cash drawer count with the over/short against the POS Z report.
  • Production: pasteurizing (185 °F for 15 seconds, or as your pasteurizer and state license say), cooling and aging the mix, batch freezing at your machine’s draw temperature, hardening at 0 °F, yield and overrun, and a shelf-life table in °F.
  • Manager and roles: daily, weekly and monthly checklists (sales by flavor, food cost, temperature logs, time records) and a checklist each for the ice cream maker, the scooper and the shift lead.
  • Cleaning and maintenance: a weekly deep clean of the dipping cabinets and the batch freezer, weekly upkeep and stock checks, monthly tasks and a quarterly and annual sheet for contracted services and paperwork, from refrigeration by an EPA 608 technician and backflow testing to your health permit and state frozen dessert license.
  • Seasons: peak season from Memorial Day to Labor Day (summer hires, the patio, pints and quarts to go), the off-season, ice cream catering, 3 blank master templates, a pre-shift huddle template and a 12-month US calendar with featured flavors, from Valentine’s Day and National Ice Cream Day to Thanksgiving.
  • The US · UK · Canada · Australia Hospitality Equivalents Guide, if you’re reading from outside the States.
