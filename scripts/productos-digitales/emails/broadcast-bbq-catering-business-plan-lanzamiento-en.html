A new product just landed in our English store: BBQ Catering Business Plan Kit. It’s for pitmasters who want to turn smoked meats into a catering company — weddings, graduation parties, corporate lunches and live-fire events — and need to know whether it works on paper before buying a smoker trailer.

It’s 4 files: a 10-section business plan in Word, an Excel financial model with 9 sheets, a pricing calculator and a 68-task startup checklist, set up for the US with notes for the UK.

Four things you’ll use from day one:

  • A plan that’s already written. Executive summary, concept and menu styles, market and competition, marketing, operations, staffing, financials, permits and fire rules, and an action plan, written for an off-site BBQ caterer that smokes at a commissary and takes the smoker trailer to weddings as a paid live pit on site, in the format lenders expect. Its figures are the ones in the Excel model, and it’s honest about them: the example is a full-time operator with a pit cook, not a typical first year.
  • Your numbers before you buy the smoker trailer. Startup costs line by line, from the smoker trailer, the live-fire rig and the used van to hot boxes, chafing dishes and permits, a 3-year P&L driven by events per year, price per guest and guests per event, break-even in events per year, three scenarios, W-2 event staff with employer payroll taxes and a 12-month cash flow with quarterly sales tax.
  • A price for every quote, by the pound. The pricing calculator turns ounces of meat per guest, your meat mix and the raw prices you pay, smoker yield, sides, wood, staff hours and mileage into a price per guest and per pound of cooked meat, adds the live pit fee, the service charge, the sales tax and the deposit, and shows the margin the quote really leaves. A meat yield and smoke schedule and a shopping list by guest count included.
  • The legal route first. 68 tasks in 6 phases: LLC and EIN, seller’s permit and business license, a written commissary agreement (no BBQ sold from your home kitchen), your county on smoking at the event, the fire marshal, Food Code temperatures for smoked meats, the trailer at the DMV with commercial auto that covers it, ServSafe Manager and food handler cards, The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola and ezCater, up to your first 90 days.

The loan schedule and the DSCR (checked against a 1.25× target) cover your main loan, from a bank or SBA 7(a); the financing sheet also lists an SBA Microloan, investors and local grants as other sources of funds. Plus three bonuses: a BBQ Catering Permits, Fire Rules & Food Safety Guide (US + UK notes), US BBQ catering benchmarks, from price per guest by package and pounds of meat per guest to smoker yield and smoker trailer prices, with their sources, and the US · UK · Canada · Australia Equivalents Guide.

The files are Microsoft Excel and Word, set up to print on US Letter. Health and fire rules vary by state and county, so the checklist starts by asking yours, and the kit is a planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice: review the final numbers with your accountant before you apply for a loan.
