A new product just landed in our English store: Food Cost + Menu Engineering Guide. It’s for anyone running a place that’s already open — owners, managers, chefs, bar managers and bakers — who has priced the menu by gut feel and wants to know, dish by dish, which one pays the bills, which one only looks good and how much the delivery apps really leave you.

It’s a 20-chapter, 95-page guide (PDF + editable DOCX) and 8 Excel workbooks with live formulas: the method to cost your recipes, price the menu and run menu engineering with the math in front of you. US edition, with notes for the UK, Canada and Australia.

Four things you’ll use from day one:

  • A food cost % on the right number. Food cost is calculated on net sales, before tax, not on a POS total with sales tax and tips still inside it. The guide handles sales tax, VAT and GST by channel, and the recipe cost card and the yield & trim loss test start your plate cost from what you actually buy, trim loss included.
  • A price for every dish, not a guess. The menu pricing calculator puts 4 methods side by side — cost factor, target dollar margin, market price and perceived value — on 20 example dishes, keeps the price from the method you choose and flags the food cost it leaves you. The guide explains when each method fits and why the cost factor breaks on high-cost dishes.
  • Which dish pays the bills. The menu engineering matrix runs Kasavana & Smith, Miller, Pavesic and Goal Value on the same menu, and a comparison sheet flags where they disagree. That’s usually where the decisions that matter are: rework, reprice, redesign or remove.
  • Delivery apps in the math. The multichannel repricing simulator adds the platform commission and the packaging per order for dine-in, takeout and third-party delivery, gives you the pre-tax price you’d need to hit your target and flags when that price won’t fit under the ceiling your market accepts.

The other three workbooks: a prime cost dashboard with food and labor together, month by month, against your target; a beverage cost menu for wine, beer, spirits and cocktails; and a 90-day action plan with an owner, a date and an estimated impact for every decision. Plus two bonuses: 12 solved exercises (32 pages), step by step, and the US · UK · Canada · Australia Equivalents Guide.

The workbooks work in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice and Numbers, in US units with metric where pros use it (bottles stay in milliliters, as on the label). Tax rates, commissions and targets live in editable cells, with tax at 0% by default because US menu prices are pre-tax: enter 20% for UK VAT or 10% for Australian GST, and in Canada you price before GST/HST, as in the US.
