A new product just landed in our English store: Paella Catering Business Plan Kit. It’s for paella chefs who want to turn giant paellas cooked live in front of the guests into a catering company — backyard paella parties, weddings, corporate events and drop-off lunches — and need to know whether it works on paper before buying pans, burners and a van.

It’s 4 files: a 10-section business plan in Word, an Excel financial model with 9 sheets, a pricing calculator and a 68-task startup checklist, set up for the US with notes for the UK.

Four things you’ll use from day one:

  • A plan that’s already written. Executive summary, concept and menu styles, market and competition, marketing, operations, staffing, financials, permits and propane rules, and an action plan, written for a live paella caterer that preps at a commissary and cooks in front of the guests, in the format lenders expect. Its figures are the ones in the Excel model, and it’s honest about them: the example is a full-time owner-operator with a second paella chef on weekends, not a typical first year.
  • Your numbers before you buy the pans and burners. Startup costs line by line, from paella pans, 3-ring propane burners and the propane kit to the used van, the canopy for the cooking station, insulated food carriers and permits, a 3-year P&L driven by events per year, price per guest and guests per event, break-even in events per year, three scenarios, W-2 event staff with employer payroll taxes and a 12-month cash flow with quarterly sales tax.
  • A price for every quote, per guest. The pricing calculator turns your package, paella style and guest count, ounces of uncooked rice per guest, the ingredient prices you pay, propane, staff hours and an optional tapas hour into the pans by size and a price per guest, adds the service charge, the sales tax and the deposit, and shows the margin the quote really leaves. A paella yield and pan sizing table and a shopping list by guest count included.
  • The legal route first. 68 tasks in 6 phases: LLC and EIN, seller’s permit and business license, a written commissary agreement (no paella sold from your home kitchen), your county on cooking at the event, the fire marshal and the propane station, cooked rice as a TCS food, shellstock tags for mussels and clams, ServSafe Manager and food handler cards, The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola and ezCater, up to your first 90 days.

The loan schedule and the DSCR (checked against a 1.25× target) cover your main loan, from a bank or SBA 7(a); the financing sheet also lists an SBA Microloan, investors and local grants as other sources of funds. Plus three bonuses: a Paella Catering Permits, Propane & Food Safety Guide (US + UK notes), US paella catering benchmarks, from price per person and guest minimums to rice per guest and guests per paella pan, with their sources or marked as kit estimates, and the US · UK · Canada · Australia Equivalents Guide.

The files are Microsoft Excel and Word, set up to print on US Letter. Health and fire rules vary by state and county, so the checklist starts by asking yours, and the kit is a planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice: review the final numbers with your accountant before you apply for a loan.
