B2     : 02 · Ice Cream & Gelato Production Checklists: Pasteurizing, Aging & Batch Freezing
B4     : How to use this template:
B5     : ▸ Checklist for the two key production phases of artisan ice cream.
B6     : ▸ Pasteurizing: mix, heat treatment, cooling and aging. Note: pasteurizing your own mix usually requires a STATE frozen dessert or dairy manufacturer license (Vermont, Georgia, Arizona…), and some states also require one to batch-freeze pasteurized mix or run a soft serve machine (Vermont, Massachusetts); with bought pasteurized mix a health permit is usually enough. Check with your state department of agriculture.
B7     : ▸ Batch freezing: texturizing, draw, hardening and storage.
B9     : Customization:
B10    : ▸ Adjust temperatures and times to your equipment and recipes.
B11    : ▸ Use the notes to log batches, yield and overrun.
B12    : ▸ Print and keep it in the production room next to the recipe cards.
B14    : Shelf life and expiry:
B15    : ▸ At the bottom of "Batch Freezing" there is an editable shelf life table by food group: adjust it to your product and supplier. It sits outside the counter because it is a reference, not a task.
B16    : ▸ Pasteurized base mix is NOT frozen: it is aged at 36–39 °F (never above 41 °F) and batch-frozen within 3–5 days (kit criterion, inside the 7-day FDA date marking, 3-501.17). What gets frozen is the finished ice cream, at 0 °F or below.
