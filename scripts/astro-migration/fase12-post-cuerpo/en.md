A yield analysis generated in the chat ended up in a Word document by hand. A catering menu proposal was copied to the email character by character. A waste table was moved to Google Sheets cell by cell. That gap between what the agent produced and the place where you actually needed to use it was small in appearance, but it added time and errors every day.

With the update we are publishing today, that gap disappears. AI Chef Pro agents have received three fundamental improvements that change the way they reason, the way they present what they generate, and the way they connect with the platforms you already use in your kitchen and your business. We'll tell you without beating around the bush.

## Agents now reason before responding

AI Chef Pro agents no longer generate the first plausible answer they find: they analyze the context, weigh the options, and consider the response before giving it to you.

This change is especially noticeable in everything that involves numbers. When you ask an agent to calculate the yield analysis of a dish, it now takes into account the actual yield of each ingredient, the cost per portion, the target margin, and possible price variations before giving you a figure. When you work on menu engineering, the agent weighs which dishes drive margin, which ones customers ask for but barely leave profit, and how to rebalance the menu sensibly. When you build a business plan for a new opening or to present to an investor, the agent cross-references the data you give it and warns you if something doesn't add up before you see it.

In practice, this means fewer answers that sound good but don't hold up to a review with a calculator. The reasoning is built into all agents; it's not an option you have to enable.

## Artifacts: what the agent generates, you can keep editing

Chat agents now create artifacts. An artifact is any self-contained content that the agent produces within the conversation and that you can keep modifying without leaving it: a text document, a data table, an interactive component, a visualization, an HTML or CSS file, a code snippet.

Instead of receiving a block of text that you have to reformat elsewhere, the agent delivers something that already has its own structure. You can ask it to modify it, add a section, change the tone, adapt it for another context, and the artifact updates in the same window. Everything stays within the conversation, versioned and accessible.

A concrete example: if you ask the agent to prepare a menu proposal for a catering event, it returns a structured document with starters, mains, desserts, allergens, and price per guest. From there you can ask it to adjust the price, remove a vegetarian option, or add wine pairing, and the document updates instantly.

**Important:** recipes and yield analyses are outside artifacts. They have their own specialized actions within the platform, designed specifically for that type of content and doing that job better. Artifacts are for everything else.

## Direct connection with 16 external platforms

Chat agents now connect with the platforms you already use, directly from the conversation, without configuration panels or integrations to set up in advance.

It works like this: you tell the agent what you want to do, in your own words, and it manages the connection. The first time you use a specific service, the agent opens the official window of that platform and asks you to authorize access with your own account. You authorize, the connection is saved, and you don't have to do it again until that authorization expires and needs renewing. AI Chef Pro never sees or stores your password.

There is no separate module to contract: it's included for all AI Chef Pro users, at no extra cost.

## Your credentials are yours: what happens with security

AI Chef Pro does not store or have access to your passwords. Management of connections with external platforms is done through Composio, the authorization layer that acts as a technical intermediary: when you authorize a platform, that authorization is stored securely and AI Chef Pro only receives a limited access token, not your credentials. Each platform manages its own authentication through its official window. If at any point you want to revoke access, you do it directly from that platform's security settings, just as you would with any other connected application.
